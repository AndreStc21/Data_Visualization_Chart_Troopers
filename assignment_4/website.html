California temperature analysis

Comparing the temperature of California

In this section, we compare the temperatures of California over the years, controlling if there is a temperature shift due to climate change.

    Last 10 years - min, max, average Temperature comparison

    This plot contains two lines representing the maximum and the minum temperature, and a scattered line, representing the average, for each year.
    Each year is represented by a colour, and for each year the minimum and maximum lines are a lighter and darker variant of the colour itself.

    On the horizontal axis there are the months, while on the vertical axis the temperature, expressed in Fahrenheit.

    Our goal is to show the increase of the temperatures over the years, but since we are considering only ten years, the shift is not so evident over the months.

    July 2024 stands out as the hottest July on record of the last ten years, with average temperatures soaring 2°F above the second maximum.

      Last 10 years - average Temperature comparison

      This polar plot displays the average monthly temperatures over multiple years, with data segmented by month and temperature expressed in degrees Fahrenheit.

      Again, each year is represented by a unique colour.
      The concentric circles represent temperature values, increasing from the center outward.

      Our goal is to show the increase of the temperatures over the years, but since we are considering only ten years, the shift is not so evident over the months.

      July 2024 stands out as the hottest July on record of the last ten years, with average temperatures soaring 2°F above the second maximum.

      Last 10 years - Temperature comparison

      This plot shows the temperature change in the last ten years.
      On the vertical axis there are the years, while on the horizontal axis there are the temperature, expressed in Fahrenheit.

      For each year we have two areas, representing the distribution of the minimum (blue) and maximum (red) average temperatures for the year.

      As we can see the temperature are slowly shifting from left (cooler temperatures) to right (hotter temperatures).
      This means that the temperatures are rising; all the months, both cooler ones, like January, and hotter ones, like July have higher and higher temperatures!

      It would have been more impactful to include additional years; however, doing so would make the chart unreadable.
